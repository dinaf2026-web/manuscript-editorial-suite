# Scoring Rubric — Grade Bands

Use these bands so a grade means the same thing every time. Resist inflation: most
strong, revisable drafts land **B- to B**. Reserve **A** for material that needs
almost nothing. Every grade needs at least one piece of textual evidence.

## Letter → number → meaning
- **A (90-100)** — Publishable as-is or near it. An editor would fight to acquire.
- **B (80-89)** — Strong; works, but has clear, fixable gaps. Home of most good drafts.
- **C (70-79)** — Promising but not yet working; a real problem in a load-bearing
  area. "Revise & resubmit" territory.
- **D (60-69)** — Significant structural or voice failure; the reader disengages.
- **F (<60)** — Actively fails the reader: confusing, inert, off-register, or
  consistency-breaking.

## Overall → Acquisitions call (hard numeric thresholds — no overlap)
Map the weighted Overall (0-100) to exactly one call. Use the number, not the
letter, so the same score always yields the same call:

- **90-100 → SIGN** (publishable as-is or near it)
- **84-89 → SIGN WITH REVISIONS** (strong; specific fixes before it goes out)
- **70-83 → PASS — REVISE & RESUBMIT** (promising; not yet competitive)
- **below 70 → PASS** (not competitive as written)

**Continuity override:** an uncorrected load-bearing continuity break caps Overall
at **C (79)** AND forces the call to **PASS — REVISE & RESUBMIT** — never SIGN or
SIGN WITH REVISIONS, no matter how high the craft scores.

**Shelf-positioning override** (Marketability is a light weight, so the Shelf Fit
Gate acts on the *call*, not just the number): if the pages drift off the intended
shelf (`genre`/`audience`/`register`), treat it as a positioning defect. An
**unresolved** shelf caps the call at **SIGN WITH REVISIONS**; a **wrong declared
shelf** caps it at **PASS — REVISE & RESUBMIT**. Apply after computing the score;
the strictest applicable override wins.

## Category weights (Overall is weighted, not averaged)
Score each category 0-100, multiply by its weight, sum, divide by 100. Continuity
Integrity is a **gate** (PASS / FLAGS), not a weighted row — FLAGS triggers the
continuity override above. Weights total 100; page-turn drivers dominate:

| Category | Weight |
|---|---|
| Voice & POV Authority | 15 |
| Hook & Opening | 13 |
| Character & Relationships | 12 |
| Plot & Structure Logic | 12 |
| Pacing & Chapter Architecture | 12 |
| Emotional Truth | 12 |
| Genre Delivery (per greatness_set) | 8 |
| Layered Stakes | 6 |
| Setting / World as Character | 5 |
| Marketability & Comps | 3 |
| Series / Standalone Architecture | 2 |
| **Total** | **100** |

**These weights are fixed for every genre — do not change them.** Reproducibility
depends on it: the same manuscript must yield the same number regardless of who runs
the pass. A genre overlay (see `greatness-markers.md`) changes only *which evidence
matters most* and the *names* of the genre-flavored rows (e.g. "Plot & Fair-Play" for
mystery, "Relationship Arc" for romance) — it never re-weights the table. If a genre
truly demands a different emphasis, express it through the category **grades** (a
romance with a flat central relationship simply scores low on Character &
Relationships and Emotional Truth), not by editing weights.

When a category is **N/A from excerpt**, drop it and renormalize the remaining
weights to 100 — and say so. Never score a category the source can't support; mark
it Provisional or N/A instead.

The VERDICT letter is derived from the same number: A 90-100, B 80-89, C 70-79,
D 60-69, F below 60.

## Per-category anchors (what an A vs. an F looks like)

**Hook & Opening** — A: page one plants a question or wrongness and a voice that
grabs; you *have* to read on. C: competent but slow; the reason to continue is
delayed. F: opens on weather, logistics, or exposition with no pull.

**Voice & POV Authority** — A: distinct, consistent, trustworthy across scenes the
narrator doesn't fully understand; appropriate to the shelf. C: mostly consistent
but slips register or over-explains. F: flat, generic, or mismatched to the reader.

**Character & Relationships** — A: distinct, dimensional cast; relationships evolve;
no one is interchangeable. C: a lead works but the supporting cast is static or one
becomes a prop. F: interchangeable characters; no relational change.

**Plot & Structure Logic** — A: every turn earned and causal; no convenience, no
coincidence doing a deduction's work. C: one convenient turn or unearned leap. F:
the structure depends on coincidence, withheld information, or out-of-character
action. (For mystery, this is the fair-play axis; for other genres, causal
plausibility and structural integrity.)

**Pacing & Chapter Architecture** — A: chapters end on open beats; relentless
forward pull. C: pull is intermittent; some chapters close fully. F: scenes that
don't advance; flat chapter ends; the reader can comfortably stop.

**Layered Stakes** — A: an immediate problem + a larger arc + a character thread
tested *in this book* all live at once. C: two layers present, the third thin. F:
single-layer (solve and go home).

**Setting / World as Character** — A: recurring sensory anchors and texture press on
the scene; couldn't be relocated. C: described but inert. F: anywhere-place;
furniture-inventory description.

**Emotional Truth** — A: the emotional spine is threaded *inside* the action beats.
C: emotion stated but not felt under the plot. F: pure mechanics; no stake beneath.

**Genre Delivery** — A: hits the core promises of its genre (see greatness-markers
for the overlay). C: delivers some, underserves others. F: doesn't deliver what its
shelf promises.

**Series / Standalone Architecture** — A: a standalone lands complete; a series book
reveals one thread and opens one question with re-readable plants. C: arc advanced
clumsily or barely. F: arc dump, or arc untouched / standalone that doesn't resolve.

**Marketability & Comps** — A: clear shelf, real recent comps it earns, legible
promise. C: market plausible but positioning fuzzy. F: no clear reader, or voice not
market-ready.

**Continuity Integrity** — PASS: no contradiction of the book's bible, no error
stated as fact. FLAGS: list each break. Any uncorrected load-bearing break caps
Overall at C.
