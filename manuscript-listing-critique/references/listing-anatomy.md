# Listing Anatomy — Converting Book Copy on KDP

Amazon's specifics (category counts, keyword mechanics, character limits) change
over time. Treat the *principles* here as durable and recommend the author confirm
any hard number in the live KDP dashboard before relying on it.

## The blurb / description structure that converts

A strong description is roughly four short movements, front-loaded:

1. **The hook (1 line).** The single most important line in the whole listing. Plant
   a question or a wrongness in the reader's mind. It is doing the work of page one
   of the book. If it reads like scene-setting, it fails.
2. **The setup (2-4 lines).** Protagonist(s) + the world + the inciting problem,
   concrete and fast. Name what makes the lead(s) compelling and ground it in the
   specific setting — place can be a selling point.
3. **The stakes & promise (2-4 lines).** What's at risk and what the reader is
   promised — the premise *and* the emotional/relationship draw the genre's reader
   wants. Raise the tension; never resolve it. Hint at the larger series arc (if
   any) for series appeal.
4. **The close (1-2 lines).** End on the dramatic question or the series promise.
   Optionally a tagline. Never the solution.

Formatting: short paragraphs, generous white space, a bolded hook or tagline,
scannable on a phone. Amazon descriptions support light HTML — use bold and breaks;
avoid a wall of text. Back-cover (print) copy is shorter and tighter than the Amazon
description; adapt, don't reuse verbatim.

## Register & voice

- Must read as the intended shelf (the profile's `genre`/`audience`/`register`):
  match the warmth, intensity, humor, or gravity the target reader expects. Flag any
  copy that accidentally markets the book to the wrong shelf (e.g. a cozy blurbed
  like a hard thriller, or a YA blurbed as middle-grade).
- Match the prose voice of the book. A breezy book with a grim blurb (or vice versa)
  mis-sets expectations and drives returns and bad reviews.
- The **comp signal** (from the profile's `comps` and `craft_standard`) is a
  positioning asset — cue the right neighborhood without copying anyone's copy.

## Hook patterns that work (adapt, don't formulate)

- A concrete missing/odd object or event ("A locket missing for thirty years turns
  up — on the wrong person's neck.").
- A safe-world-cracked beat ("This town keeps its secrets. She keeps finding them.").
- The character/pair hook ("She sees everything. He notices what she can't.").
Avoid: abstract stakes ("a secret that could change everything"), throat-clearing
geography, or synopsis ("In this book, the protagonist…").

## Categories (Amazon)

- Choose the most **specific, accurate, reachable** category paths — a high rank in a
  precise subcategory beats invisibility in a giant one. Pick paths that match the
  actual genre and age register.
- Amazon allows multiple categories per title (the exact count and request mechanism
  change — verify in KDP). Use available slots on distinct, on-target paths, not
  near-duplicates.
- Avoid categories that over-promise a different (older/grittier/younger) read than
  the book is.

## Keywords (backend slots)

- Use **buyer-intent phrases**, not single generic words: e.g. "small town cozy
  mystery series", "enemies to lovers fantasy romance", "literary coming of age
  novel".
- Don't waste slots repeating the title, author, or chosen category terms — already
  indexed.
- No keyword stuffing, competitor author names, or unsubstantiated claims
  ("bestselling") — KDP compliance risk.
- Think in reader search language: how a reader, parent, or gift-buyer would type the
  search, including the subgenre, age, and comp neighborhood.

## KDP compliance constraints (flag violations)

Amazon enforces metadata rules; a violation can suppress or block a listing.
Specifics shift — verify in the live KDP dashboard — but the durable constraints:

- **Reading age / primary audience** set and consistent with the intended shelf — a
  young-protagonist book listed as adult, or an adult novel listed as YA,
  mis-targets.
- **Primary marketplace** appropriate for the audience.
- **Categories must not be misleading** — no placing a book in an unrelated or
  mismatched category to chase rank.
- **Keywords:** no other authors' names or other book titles; no promotional claims
  ("free", "bestseller", "on sale", "award-winning" unless true and permitted); no
  HTML; no profanity; don't duplicate title, subtitle, series name, author, or
  chosen-category terms (already indexed — wasted slots).
- **Description:** only light, supported HTML where Amazon allows it; no unverifiable
  claims; no reviews, testimonials, or quotes attributed to others; no URLs, contact
  info, or requests for reviews; no promotional or time-sensitive claims; no keyword-
  or tag-stuffing (the description is sales copy, not a keyword field).

## Read-through & series

- Self-pub economics reward **read-through**: if it's a series, the listing should
  make clear there's momentum (the larger arc, "Book Two") so a Book 1 buyer knows
  there's more.
- Consistent series branding (byline, cover system, "Series Name, Book N" subtitle)
  supports discoverability and trust.

## Quick conversion checklist

- [ ] First line hooks, doesn't set up.
- [ ] Concrete details, not abstract stakes.
- [ ] The lead(s) and their draw present.
- [ ] Right register (matches the shelf) and matches the book's voice.
- [ ] Spoiler-safe; honors `marketing_constraints`; ends on the dramatic question.
- [ ] Scannable formatting.
- [ ] Categories specific, accurate, reachable.
- [ ] Keywords are buyer-intent phrases, no waste, no compliance risk.
- [ ] Series promise visible for read-through (if a series).
