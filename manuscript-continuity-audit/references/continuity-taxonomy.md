# Continuity Taxonomy — Defect Types & Drift-Prone Details

Run the manuscript against this layer. The book's own bible (set in the project
profile's `canon_source`) remains the full authority; when the bible and the
manuscript disagree, the bible wins unless the author changed canon. With no
bible, use this to drive an internal-consistency audit.

## Defect types (classify each finding as one of these)

1. **Load-bearing break (blocking).** Contradicts a fact the story or a character
   depends on — a secret exposed, a death undone, a timeline the plot needs. Caps
   any later craft grade until fixed.
2. **Continuity drift.** A previously established detail rendered differently —
   eye color, a habit, a room's layout, a vehicle, a title. Individually small;
   collectively they erode trust.
3. **Internal inconsistency.** The text contradicts *itself* regardless of any
   bible — a character in two places at once, an object both lost and present, a
   season that changes mid-scene.
4. **Intentional evolution.** A difference that is on purpose (a character ages, a
   relationship shifts, a secret deepens). NOT a defect — but worth surfacing so
   the author confirms it and logs it.
5. **New canon (unlogged).** A permissible new detail not yet in the bible. Not a
   defect; flag it as something to add to canon.
6. **Unverifiable.** A detail the bible is silent on that you can't confirm or
   deny. Never guess — route to the author.

## Drift-prone detail categories (where continuity errors hide)

Most continuity breaks cluster in these recurring categories. Check each.

### Character physicality & identity
Hair, eyes, height, build, scars/marks, age, dominant hand, distinctive clothing
or props. Names, nicknames, surnames, titles, and relationships (who is whose
parent/sibling/partner). A surname or relationship error reads as a different
person.

### Character behavior & voice
Verbal tics, signature lines, habits, tells (what a character does when lying,
nervous, angry). A behavioral tell that's load-bearing for the plot (a lie
detected by a tell) must stay consistent.

### Timeline & sequence
Ages at events, dates, days of the week, durations, the order things happened,
how much time passes between scenes, character ages relative to each other.
Flashbacks and backstory are the highest-risk zone.

### Locations & geography
Layout of recurring places (which door, which window, what's on the desk), travel
times and directions, the relationship of places to each other, real-world
geography if the setting is real (must be accurate or use a fictional equivalent —
never present invented real-world fact as true; flag if unsure).

### World rules & systems
How the magic/technology/institution/profession works and its limits. Established
costs, constraints, and exceptions. A rule the plot relies on cannot bend for
convenience without becoming a fairness break.

### Objects & possessions
Who owns what, where a significant object is at any time, its description, whether
it's been destroyed/lost/transferred. The "object both lost and present" error.

### Established facts from prior chapters/books
Anything stated as true earlier — a backstory beat, a prior revelation, a
relationship status, a stated motive. Cross-book series facts are the densest
source of drift; a living canon ledger exists to track them.

## Factual-accuracy traps (flag, don't guess)

- Real geography, landmarks, institutions, or history must be accurate, or use a
  clearly fictional equivalent. If unsure, flag — never state invented real-world
  fact as true.
- Specialized detail (medical, legal, police procedure, technical, period detail)
  must be accurate or flagged for the author/expert to verify.

## How to report

For every finding give: the defect type, the established value (or the two
conflicting statements), the deviation, the location (chapter/scene/quote anchor),
and — for drift — the value to restore. Never invent a canon value to "fix" the
text; if the bible is silent, it's Unverifiable / Needs Author.
